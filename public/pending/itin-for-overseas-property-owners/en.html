Living in Taiwan with a San Diego home: do you need an ITIN?

Updated November 23, 2026 · QY Property

An ITIN (Individual Taxpayer Identification Number) is a number the IRS issues to people who have a U.S. federal tax purpose but are not eligible for a Social Security number (SSN). It is for federal tax purposes only. If you live in Taiwan and own a San Diego home, you will usually need one once you report rental income to the IRS or file a return after selling. You apply with Form W-7, sent together with a federal tax return and identity documents; a passport is the only stand-alone document. The IRS usually replies in about 7 weeks, or 9 to 11 weeks in tax season or when you apply from overseas.

What is an ITIN, and what doesn't it do?

From the IRS ITIN page:

  • Federal tax only: the IRS issues ITINs for federal tax purposes. Anyone with a federal tax purpose who is not eligible for an SSN can apply, regardless of immigration status.
  • What it doesn't do: an ITIN does not qualify you for Social Security benefits, does not provide or change immigration status, does not authorize work in the U.S., and is not identification outside the federal tax system.
  • Who doesn't need one: U.S. citizens, green card holders, and anyone eligible for an SSN (for example, people on a U.S. work visa). You can't have both an ITIN and an SSN.

When does a San Diego home owner need an ITIN?

What matters is whether you have something to report to the IRS. For owners who live in Taiwan and have no SSN, the common cases are:

  • you rent the home out and report the rental income to the IRS;
  • you sell the home and file a return on the sale.

Buying a home does not by itself require an ITIN first. Whether you need to file, and how, is a question for a CPA. For who does what when you buy from Taiwan, see Buying a San Diego home from Taiwan.

How do you apply, and what do you send?

According to the IRS application page, the package has three parts:

  1. Form W-7 (the ITIN application);
  2. a U.S. federal income tax return: attach the W-7 to it and send them together, not separately, on or before the return's due date;
  3. supporting documents (next section).

The IRS also says you can apply at any time if you are filing a prior-year return or meet an exception to the filing requirement; the exceptions are listed in the Instructions for Form W-7. Ask a CPA whether one applies to you.

Which identity documents does the IRS accept?

From the IRS supporting documents page:

  • A passport is the only stand-alone document, and it must not be expired when you submit the W-7. Without a valid passport you need two documents, one proving identity and one proving foreign status, and one must have a photo.
  • Documents must be originals or certified copies made by the issuing agency. The IRS does not accept notarized copies (with narrow exceptions, such as dependents of U.S. military personnel overseas).
  • Documents mailed to the IRS are returned to the mailing address on line 2 of your W-7 within 60 days, so your original passport may be away for a while.

How can you apply from Taiwan?

The IRS application page lists these options:

How long does it take?

The IRS says to allow 7 weeks to hear about your application, and 9 to 11 weeks in tax season (January 15 to April 30) or if you apply from overseas. If you live in Taiwan, start early rather than close to a filing deadline.

Does an ITIN expire?

Yes. From the IRS renewal page:

  • If an ITIN isn't used on a U.S. federal tax return for 3 consecutive tax years, it expires on December 31 of the third year.
  • An expired ITIN must be renewed before it goes on a federal return; you renew with Form W-7, checking "Renew an existing ITIN".
  • An ITIN used only on information returns such as Form 1099 can still be used there after it expires; you don't need to renew it for that.

If you file every year while the home is rented, the ITIN stays in use. If you skip a few years, check that it is still valid before you file or sell.

Frequently asked questions

What is an ITIN?

An ITIN is a number the IRS issues to people who have a U.S. federal tax purpose but are not eligible for a Social Security number. It is for federal tax purposes only; it does not give immigration status, work authorization or Social Security benefits.

Do I need an ITIN to own a San Diego home while living in Taiwan?

Not necessarily. Buying does not by itself require one, but if you report rental income to the IRS or file a return after selling, and you have no SSN, you will usually need an ITIN. Check your situation with a CPA.

What do I need to apply for an ITIN?

Form W-7, a U.S. federal tax return with the W-7 attached, and identity documents. A passport is the only stand-alone document; documents must be originals or certified copies from the issuing agency, and the IRS does not accept notarized copies. People who meet an exception can apply without a return.

How long does an ITIN take from Taiwan?

The IRS says about 7 weeks, or 9 to 11 weeks during tax season (January 15 to April 30) or when you apply from overseas.

Does an ITIN expire?

Yes. If it is not used on a federal tax return for 3 consecutive tax years, it expires on December 31 of the third year. You renew it with Form W-7 before using it on a return again.
