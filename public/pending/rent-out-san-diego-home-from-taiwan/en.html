Renting Out Your San Diego Home While You Live in Taiwan: Management and Tax

Updated November 20, 2026 · QY Property

Yes. You can rent out a San Diego home while living in Taiwan, usually with two professionals: a property manager finds tenants, collects rent and arranges repairs, and a CPA who knows nonresident filing handles the U.S. tax. On tax, know one thing first: according to the IRS, U.S. rent received by a nonresident alien is generally taxed at 30% of the gross rent, with no deductions; or you can elect under IRC 871(d) to be taxed on the net income after expenses, and then file Form 1040-NR every year. Which fits you is a question for your CPA.

Can you rent out a San Diego home if you live in Taiwan?

Yes. The owner doesn't have to live in the U.S. to rent a home out. What needs arranging is who handles the day-to-day: showings, screening tenants, signing the lease, collecting rent, who to call when a pipe leaks at night, and the yearly tax filing. That is hard to do from Taiwan, so most overseas owners hire a property manager and leave the tax to a CPA.

How does the U.S. tax a nonresident's rental income?

According to the IRS, income from U.S. real property owned by a nonresident alien is generally taxed at 30% if it is not effectively connected with a U.S. trade or business. The IRS also explains that this 30% applies to the gross amount, with no deductions. So under this method, management fees, repairs and property tax don't reduce the tax.

What is the 871(d) election?

The IRS lets a nonresident alien who holds U.S. real property to produce income elect, under IRC 871(d), to treat the rent as income effectively connected with a U.S. trade or business. With the election:

  • You can deduct expenses connected with the property income, and the net income is taxed at graduated rates.
  • You make the first election with a statement attached to Form 1040-NR (or an amended return, Form 1040-X).
  • You give Form W-8ECI to whoever pays you the rent (for example your property manager).
  • While the election is in effect, you file Form 1040-NR every year.
  • The election stays in effect; revoking it has time limits, and after them it needs IRS approval.

See the IRS page for the details. Whether and when to make the election is for your CPA to judge for your situation.

Do you need an ITIN to rent it out?

Filing Form 1040-NR usually needs a taxpayer number. People who aren't eligible for a Social Security number can apply to the IRS for an ITIN (Form W-7; see the IRS ITIN page). For ITIN and FIRPTA when you buy, see Buying a San Diego home while you live in Taiwan.

How do you choose a property manager?

When you meet a property manager (or talk by video), ask these first:

  1. What do the fees cover? The monthly fee, the fee for placing a new tenant, lease renewals, and whether repairs carry a mark-up.
  2. Up to what amount can they approve repairs? Agree a limit in advance; above it they ask you first, so a bill never surprises you in Taiwan.
  3. How often do you get statements? Monthly income and expenses, repair photos and invoices, ideally online.
  4. How is the rent paid to you? To a U.S. or a Taiwan account, how often, and who pays the transfer fees.
  5. How do they handle the tax forms? For example the Form W-8ECI you give them if you make the 871(d) election.
  6. Licence. If they say they hold a California real estate licence, check it on the California Department of Real Estate (DRE) public licence lookup.

Frequently asked questions

Do I have to file U.S. tax on San Diego rent if I live in Taiwan?

Generally yes. According to the IRS, a nonresident alien's U.S. rental income is generally taxed at 30% of the gross rent without the 871(d) election; with the election it is taxed on net income and you file Form 1040-NR every year. Check your situation with a CPA.

Is the 30% on the gross rent or after expenses?

Without the 871(d) election it is on the gross amount, with no deductions. With the election you can deduct expenses connected with the property income and are taxed on the net.

Can I cancel the 871(d) election at any time?

No. According to the IRS the election stays in effect; you can revoke it with Form 1040-X within a time limit (3 years from filing the original return or 2 years from paying the tax, whichever is later), and after that only with IRS approval.

Who handles urgent repairs while I am in Taiwan?

Your property manager, under your agreement with them. Agree a limit in advance: repairs under it go ahead, anything above it needs your OK first.
