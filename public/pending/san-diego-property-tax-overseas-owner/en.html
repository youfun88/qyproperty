San Diego property tax when you live in Taiwan: how it works and how to pay

Updated November 22, 2026 · QY Property

A San Diego home comes with a yearly property tax bill. According to the San Diego County Treasurer-Tax Collector, California's Proposition 13 limits the rate to 1% of the property's assessed value, plus any voter-approved bonds and assessments. The annual bill is paid in two installments: the first is due November 1 and delinquent after December 10; the second is due February 1 and delinquent after April 10. Penalties are not waived because a bill never reached you in Taiwan, so you need to track the dates and your mailing address yourself.

How is San Diego property tax calculated?

According to the Treasurer-Tax Collector:

  • Rate: Proposition 13 limits the tax rate to 1% of the property's current assessed value, plus any voter-approved bonds and assessments. The total rate therefore differs by location; your bill shows the actual amount.
  • Values rise at most 2% a year: based on the California Consumer Price Index.
  • Reassessment on sale or new construction: the property is reassessed whenever it changes owners or undergoes new construction.

The County Assessor sets the assessed value; the Treasurer-Tax Collector sends the bills and collects the tax. They are separate offices.

When is property tax due?

According to the Treasurer-Tax Collector, secured property tax bills are mailed in late September and paid in two installments:

InstallmentDueDelinquentLate penalty
FirstNovember 1After close of business December 1010%
SecondFebruary 1After close of business April 1010% plus a $10 cost

If a delinquent date falls on a weekend or holiday, it moves to the next business day. You can also pay both installments when the first is due.

I just bought. Why did I get a "supplemental" tax bill?

According to the Treasurer-Tax Collector on supplemental taxes, state law requires the County to reassess a property when it is sold to a new owner or new construction is completed. When the assessed value changes, the County sends a separate supplemental tax bill:

  • It taxes the difference between the old value and the new market value, prorated from the day you closed escrow to the end of the fiscal year (June 30), at the 1% rate.
  • Supplemental bills are separate from and in addition to the annual bills, and they are not sent to a lender for payment. The owner must make sure they are paid on time.
  • Paying late adds a 10% penalty, plus $10 on a late second installment.
  • If the new value is lower than the prior one, you get a negative supplemental bill with a refund.

So in the first year after buying, budget for a supplemental bill as well as the annual one.

What if the bill never reaches me in Taiwan?

The Treasurer-Tax Collector is direct about this: penalties will not be waived due to not receiving a bill, and it is the taxpayer's responsibility to obtain all tax bills and pay on time. New owners are told not to wait to be notified, because a bill may never come.

  • Change your address with the Assessor, not the Tax Collector: the Tax Collector asks owners to keep the Assessor, Recorder, County Clerk (ARCC) informed of any change of address (619-236-3771).
  • Look up your bills yourself: the Tax Collector's site lets you search for and pay secured, supplemental and other bills by parcel number or address.

How can I pay from Taiwan?

According to the Treasurer-Tax Collector, the fastest way is an e-check through its online payment system, which is free; payment is complete only once you click "Submit" and see a "Successful" receipt. It also warns that if you are outside the U.S. and get access errors, your country may be blocked: mail the payment (postmarked by the delinquent date) or pay by phone at (855) 829-3773.

Does the Homeowners' Exemption apply to me?

According to the California State Board of Equalization, the California Constitution provides a $7,000 reduction in the taxable value of a qualifying owner-occupied home; the home must be the owner's principal place of residence on January 1. If you live in Taiwan and the home is not your principal residence, you generally won't meet that condition. If a family member lives there or the ownership is unusual, check with the County Assessor or a CPA.

Frequently asked questions

What is the property tax rate in San Diego?

According to the San Diego County Treasurer-Tax Collector, Proposition 13 limits the rate to 1% of the assessed value, plus any voter-approved bonds and assessments, so the total rate varies by location; your bill shows the amount. Values can rise at most 2% a year and are reassessed on a change of owner or new construction.

When is San Diego property tax due?

Annual bills are mailed in late September and paid in two installments: the first is due November 1 and delinquent after December 10; the second is due February 1 and delinquent after April 10. A late installment adds a 10% penalty, plus $10 on the second.

I live in Taiwan and never got the bill. Is the penalty waived?

No. The Treasurer-Tax Collector says penalties are not waived for not receiving a bill; getting the bill and paying on time is the owner's responsibility. Report address changes to the Assessor (ARCC) and look up your bills on the Tax Collector's website.

Why did I get a supplemental tax bill after buying?

When a property changes owners or new construction is completed, the County reassesses it and bills the difference between the old and new value, prorated from the close of escrow to June 30. Supplemental bills are not sent to your lender; the owner pays them.

Can I claim the Homeowners' Exemption if I live in Taiwan?

The Homeowners' Exemption ($7,000 off the taxable value) applies only if the home is the owner's principal place of residence on January 1, according to the State Board of Equalization. An owner living in Taiwan generally does not qualify. Check unusual cases with the County Assessor or a CPA.
