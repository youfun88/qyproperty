Buying a San Diego Home While You Live in Taiwan: Steps and Who Does What

Updated November 19, 2026 · QY Property

Yes. You can buy a San Diego home while living in Taiwan: tours can be done by video, and documents can be signed and notarized in Taiwan and sent back, so many steps don't need a trip to the U.S. Each step still belongs to the right professional: a California-licensed real estate agent represents you in the offer and contract, an escrow company usually holds the funds and handles the closing, a CPA handles U.S. tax questions, and an attorney advises on how to hold the property. QY Property helps you plan in Mandarin and English and coordinates these professionals.

Can you buy a San Diego home if you don't live in the U.S.?

Yes. You don't have to live in the U.S. to buy a home in San Diego. What you do need to work out is how the money moves from Taiwan, whose name or which structure the home is held in, and how you will file taxes if you rent it out or sell it later. It is best to settle these questions before you start looking, because changing how a property is held afterwards is usually harder.

What are the steps to buy remotely?

  1. Set the goal and budget. Long-term rental, a future home for family, or both? The goal shapes the location, the type of home and how you hold it.
  2. Talk to a CPA and an attorney about how to hold it. Holding in your own name, through an LLC or in a trust differs for tax, liability and estate planning; which fits depends on your family and is for those professionals to advise on.
  3. Choose a California-licensed real estate agent. Before signing, confirm the licence on the California Department of Real Estate (DRE) public licence lookup.
  4. Tour by video and get an inspection. Your agent can show homes online; after an offer, a professional inspector usually checks the home and the report can be emailed to you.
  5. Make an offer and sign. Your agent represents you in the offer and negotiation; contracts can be signed electronically.
  6. During escrow: funds and documents. The deposit and the balance are wired to the escrow company. Documents you sign in Taiwan need to be notarized, so ask the escrow or title company in advance which notarization they accept. Before any wire, call the escrow company on a phone number you already know to confirm the wiring details; never rely on account details in an email alone.
  7. After closing. Arrange insurance, property management and yearly tax filing.

Do foreign buyers need an ITIN?

An ITIN (Individual Taxpayer Identification Number) is a 9-digit number the IRS issues to people who need a U.S. taxpayer number for federal tax purposes but aren't eligible for a Social Security number. Buying a home doesn't always require one first, but you will usually need one to report U.S. rental income or to file when you sell. You apply with Form W-7; see the IRS ITIN page. Ask your CPA when to apply.

What is FIRPTA when you sell later?

FIRPTA is the U.S. withholding rule for foreign persons who sell U.S. real property. According to the IRS, when a foreign person sells, the buyer generally must withhold 15% of the amount realized and pay it to the IRS. That is not the final tax: the seller settles it on their tax return and can claim back any excess, and can apply to the IRS in advance for a withholding certificate to reduce the amount withheld.

Some sales are exempt, for example when the buyer buys the home to live in, the price is not more than $300,000, and the residence conditions are met (see the IRS FIRPTA exceptions). Knowing about FIRPTA when you buy avoids surprises when you sell. Confirm which rules apply to you with a CPA.

Frequently asked questions

Do I have to fly to the U.S. to buy a San Diego home?

Not necessarily. Tours can be done by video, inspection reports and contracts can be handled electronically, and documents that need notarizing can be signed in Taiwan and sent back. Confirm with your agent and escrow company whether anything needs you in person.

How do I check that an agent is licensed in California?

Use the public licence lookup on the California Department of Real Estate (DRE) website. Enter the name or licence number to see the status, expiry date and the responsible broker.

Do I have to set up an LLC to buy?

No. Holding in your own name, through an LLC or in a trust each has trade-offs for tax, liability and estate planning; an attorney and a CPA can advise which fits your situation.

Is the 15% FIRPTA withholding the final tax on a sale?

No. The 15% is withheld in advance, not the final tax. The seller settles the actual tax on their return and can claim back any excess.
