Selling a San Diego home while living in Taiwan: what is FIRPTA withholding?

Updated November 21, 2026 · QY Property

Yes. According to the IRS, when a foreign person sells U.S. real estate, the buyer generally withholds 15% of the amount realized and sends it to the IRS. This is FIRPTA withholding. It is withholding, not necessarily your final tax: the seller files a U.S. income tax return with the IRS-stamped Form 8288-A to claim it, and any excess is refunded. You can also apply on Form 8288-B before closing to have less withheld. Talk to a CPA who works with nonresidents about what fits your case.

What is FIRPTA, and why is 15% withheld when I sell?

FIRPTA is the U.S. rule for foreign persons who sell U.S. real property. Per the IRS, the buyer (transferee) must find out whether the seller is a foreign person; if so, the buyer withholds tax from the amount realized, generally at 15%. An owner who lives in Taiwan and is not a U.S. tax resident is usually in this position when selling.

15% of what?

Of the amount realized, not the gain. Per the IRS, the amount realized includes:

  • the cash paid (principal only);
  • the fair market value of any other property transferred;
  • any liabilities the buyer assumes or that the property is subject to.

So even if you made little or no profit on the home, 15% of the whole amount realized is withheld. That is why many foreign sellers ask a CPA to estimate the real tax before they list.

When is nothing withheld?

The IRS lists several exceptions. The two most relevant for a typical owner in Taiwan:

  • The amount realized is $300,000 or less and the buyer will live in the home. The buyer must be an individual, and the buyer or a family member must have definite plans to live there at least 50% of the days it is used in each of the first two 12-month periods after the sale.
  • The buyer receives a withholding certificate from the IRS that excuses withholding. See the next question.

Can I ask for less to be withheld before closing?

Yes. Per the IRS page on withholding certificates, the buyer or the seller can apply on Form 8288-B, for example when 15% would be more than the seller's maximum tax. Keep in mind:

  • The IRS generally acts within 90 days of receiving a complete application, including everyone's taxpayer identification numbers, so start early.
  • The seller must tell the buyer in writing, on the day of or the day before the transfer, that an application has been made.
  • Per the IRS, if the application is still pending at closing, the buyer must still withhold the 15%, but can pay it within 20 days after the IRS mails the certificate or a notice of denial.

How do I get excess withholding back?

Per the IRS, the IRS stamps Copy B of Form 8288-A and sends it to the seller. The seller must file a U.S. income tax return and attach the stamped 8288-A to get credit for the tax withheld; if more was withheld than the tax owed, the difference is refunded. Nonresident individuals generally file Form 1040-NR (see the IRS page on nonresidents and U.S. real property). So in the year you sell, plan on filing a U.S. return.

Do I need an ITIN to sell?

Per the IRS, the FIRPTA forms must show the buyer's and the seller's taxpayer identification numbers. Without the seller's number, the IRS does not send the stamped 8288-A; the seller must attach other evidence such as closing documents instead, which is slower. If you are not eligible for an SSN, you can apply for an ITIN on Form W-7 (see the IRS ITIN page); the IRS also allows a completed Form 8288-B to be attached to the W-7.

What should I prepare before selling from Taiwan?

  1. Tell your agent and escrow that you are a foreign seller. That way FIRPTA withholding is part of the plan from day one.
  2. Ask a CPA to estimate the actual tax. Have your original purchase price, closing documents and receipts for improvements ready.
  3. Decide whether to apply on Form 8288-B. If 15% is clearly more than the real tax, consider applying early; the IRS generally needs up to 90 days.
  4. Check that you have an ITIN. If not, apply with your CPA.
  5. Plan the tax return after the sale. You file a U.S. income tax return for the year of the sale to claim the withholding or get the excess back.

Frequently asked questions

Is 15% always withheld when a foreign owner sells a U.S. home?

Generally, yes. Per the IRS, when a foreign person sells U.S. real property, the buyer generally withholds 15% of the amount realized. Exceptions include a sale of $300,000 or less to an individual buyer who will live in the home, or a withholding certificate from the IRS that excuses withholding.

Is the 15% the final tax?

Not necessarily. The 15% is withholding on the sale price, not on the gain. The seller files a U.S. income tax return with the IRS-stamped Form 8288-A to get credit for the amount withheld, and any excess is refunded. Ask a CPA to work out the actual tax from your purchase price and costs.

Can I ask for less to be withheld before closing?

Yes. The buyer or the seller can apply to the IRS for a withholding certificate on Form 8288-B. The IRS generally acts within 90 days of receiving a complete application, including everyone's taxpayer identification numbers, so start early with your CPA.

Do I need an ITIN to sell?

Per the IRS, the FIRPTA forms must show the buyer's and the seller's taxpayer identification numbers. Without one, the IRS does not send the seller a stamped Form 8288-A, which makes getting credit slower. People not eligible for an SSN can apply for an ITIN on Form W-7.
