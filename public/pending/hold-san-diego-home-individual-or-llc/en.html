Buying a San Diego home from Taiwan: hold it in your own name or through an LLC?

Updated November 24, 2026 · QY Property

Both are used, and neither is better for every family. Holding the home in your own name is the simplest: the title is in your name, and you report to the IRS yourself when you rent or sell. Holding it through a California LLC means registering with the California Secretary of State and then paying at least $800 a year in California LLC annual tax and filing with the Franchise Tax Board (FTB) every year (FTB). How the IRS treats an LLC depends on how many members it has and on any election it makes (IRS). The choice touches liability, tax, financing and future transfers, so talk to a US attorney and a CPA before you sign. Below are the basic rules from the official websites.

What does holding the home in your own name mean?

The title is in your name (or yours and your family's). If you rent it out, the IRS page on US real property owned by nonresident aliens says:

  • in general, the rent is taxed at 30% of the gross amount, with no deductions;
  • you can instead elect under section 871(d) to be taxed on net income: you deduct the expenses of the property and pay tax at graduated rates. Once you make the election you file Form 1040-NR every year until you revoke it;
  • someone who has to file with the IRS and has no Social Security number usually needs an ITIN first.

Which way fits you is a question for a CPA.

How does the IRS treat an LLC?

An LLC (limited liability company) is created under state law. According to the IRS, it is treated as one of these, depending on its members and elections:

SituationFederal tax treatment
One member, no electionA "disregarded entity": its income and expenses go on the owner's own tax return.
Two or more members, no electionA partnership.
Elects corporate treatment on Form 8832Taxed as a corporation.

The IRS has separate rules for an LLC owned by a married couple; ask a CPA. The FTB adds that an LLC must have the same classification for California and federal tax purposes (FTB).

What does a California LLC cost each year?

From the FTB pages on LLCs and single member LLCs:

  • Registration: you form a California LLC by filing Articles of Organization (Form LLC-1) with the California Secretary of State.
  • $800 annual tax: every LLC doing business or organized in California pays an $800 annual tax each year, even if it does no business, until the LLC is cancelled. The first payment is due by the 15th day of the 4th month after you file with the Secretary of State.
  • LLC fee on higher income: an LLC with total California income of $250,000 or more also pays an LLC fee set by income band (amounts on the FTB site).
  • Form 568 every year: California requires a single member LLC to file Form 568 even though it is disregarded for federal tax purposes.

These are the state's fixed costs only; attorney, CPA and other service fees come on top.

What does an LLC protect?

The FTB describes an LLC as something you can form to run a business or to hold assets, and says LLCs protect their members against personal liabilities (FTB). How far that protection goes, its exceptions and how to keep it are legal questions for a US attorney.

How do you decide? Questions to take to an attorney and a CPA

  1. How many people will own the home? What changes if a couple, or parents and a child, own it together?
  2. Will the home be lived in (for example by a child studying in the US) or rented out? How does reporting differ between owning it personally and through an LLC?
  3. If you need a loan, will the lender lend to an LLC?
  4. If you later sell the home or pass it to your children, how do the steps and taxes differ?
  5. If you already bought in your own name, should you, and can you, move it into an LLC later? What should you watch for?
  6. Is the LLC's annual California tax, filing and upkeep worth what it gives you?

Frequently asked questions

Do I need an LLC to buy a San Diego home from Taiwan?

No. The home can be titled in your own name or held through an LLC. Which fits you depends on liability, tax, financing and future transfers, so ask a US attorney and a CPA before you sign.

How much does a California LLC pay the state each year?

According to the California Franchise Tax Board, every LLC doing business or organized in California pays at least an $800 annual tax, even with no business, until it is cancelled; an LLC with total California income of $250,000 or more also pays an LLC fee. It files Form 568 every year.

How does the IRS treat a single-member LLC?

According to the IRS, an LLC with one member that has not elected corporate treatment on Form 8832 is a disregarded entity: its income and expenses go on the owner's return. An LLC with two or more members is a partnership.

If I own the home in my own name and rent it out, how is the rent taxed?

According to the IRS, US real property income of a nonresident alien is generally taxed at 30% of the gross amount. You can elect under section 871(d) to be taxed on net income at graduated rates and then file Form 1040-NR every year. Ask a CPA which fits you.
